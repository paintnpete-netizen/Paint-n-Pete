# The Review Engine

**The highest-return work in the business.** 300+ completed projects, 14 public
reviews. Reviews are the strongest single factor in local ranking and in whether
someone calls you instead of the next painter. Nothing else in this back office
returns as much for as little effort.

If 40% of your last 30 clients respond, you land near 26 new reviews and roughly
triple your public count in a few weeks.

---

## The rules

1. **Ask at peak satisfaction** — standing in the finished room at final
   walkthrough. Not by email a week later, when the feeling has faded.
2. **Ask out loud first, then text the link within the hour**, while you're
   still fresh in their mind. The verbal ask is what makes the text land.
3. **One follow-up, two days later. One.** Never a third. A third request costs
   you more goodwill than the review is worth.
4. **Respond to every review within 48 hours.** Google notices, and so does
   every person reading them.
5. **Never offer anything in exchange for a review.** It violates Google's
   policy and can get your reviews removed or the profile penalised. The ask is
   free or it isn't made.

---

## Pace the backlog — this matters

Do **not** text all 30 past clients on the same afternoon.

Google's spam filtering looks at review velocity. A profile that sat at 14 for
years and takes 20 reviews in 48 hours can have the burst filtered out — and
filtered reviews are very hard to recover. You'd be doing the highest-value work
in the business and then watching it get deleted.

**Send 5 a day over about six days**, oldest clients first. Roughly a review
every day or two after that, which reads as organic because it is.

---

## Message 1 — evening of the final walkthrough

> Noah here. Thanks again for having us in the house — the [SPECIFIC DETAIL, e.g.
> "stair rail" / "kitchen cabinets"] came out the way I hoped it would. If you
> were happy with the work, a Google review helps more than you'd guess. Takes
> about a minute: [REVIEW_LINK]

Fill in the specific detail every time. It's the difference between a message
that reads as personal and one that reads as a blast. The whole ask is carried
by that one clause.

---

## Message 2 — two days later, if nothing yet

> Following up once and then I'll leave you alone. If you have a minute for a
> Google review, it genuinely helps people find us: [REVIEW_LINK]. Either way,
> thanks for the work — enjoy the [ROOM / HOUSE].

"Then I'll leave you alone" does real work. It removes the worry that saying
nothing means being asked forever, and it's true, so honour it.

---

## Message 3 — past clients, months or years on

> Noah Kanwal here, Paint'n Pete — we painted your [PROJECT] back in [MONTH,
> YEAR]. Late to be asking, but I'm finally getting our reviews caught up with
> our work. If you were happy with how it held up, a Google review would mean a
> lot: [REVIEW_LINK]

Naming the delay defuses it. "Caught up with our work" is also the honest
reason, and for exterior clients "how it held up" quietly invites the durability
comment — which is the most persuasive kind of review a painter can have.

## Responding to reviews

### Five-star and detailed

> Thank you, [NAME] — and thanks for noticing the prep. That's the part that
> decides how the [SURFACE] looks in five years, and the part nobody sees. One
> of the better [NEIGHBOURHOOD] houses we've worked on. Call anytime.

### Five-star, one line

> Thanks, [NAME]. Good working with you here in St. Pete — glad the [ROOM /
> EXTERIOR] turned out the way you wanted. If anything needs attention down the
> road, you know where to find us.

### Three-star — the job ran longer than expected

> [NAME], you're right that it ran longer than I told you it would, and that's
> on me. [SPECIFIC: e.g. "Humidity pushed the cure times out, but I should have
> built that into the schedule I gave you."] I'd rather hear the rest directly —
> 727-902-1986. Thanks for the honest feedback.

Take responsibility for the schedule, not for the craft. Name the real cause,
then own the fact that you should have priced the risk into what you promised.
Never argue in public and never explain at length — a short reply that accepts
the point reads as confidence to everyone else scrolling past. The phone number
moves it off the profile, which is where you want it.

## Workflow

**Going forward, every job.** Ask verbally at the walkthrough. Text Message 1
that evening. Log it. Message 2 in two days if nothing appears. Stop there.

**The backlog, once.** Work `review-tracker.csv`, five a day, oldest first.

**Every review, within 48 hours.** Reply using the templates above.

Once the Sheets are set up, workflow 4 in `../07-automation/` fires the
walkthrough-evening reminder and the two-day follow-up automatically, so this
stops depending on you remembering it at the end of a long day.
