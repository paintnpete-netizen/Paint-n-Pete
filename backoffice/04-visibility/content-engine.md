# Content Engine

The goal is not to become a content creator. The goal is thirty minutes on a
Monday that produces a week of posts, so the profile and the feed do not go
quiet between jobs.

**The rule:** every post comes from a real job. If you didn't photograph
anything last week, you post nothing. Making things up is worse than silence.

---

## Weekly batch prompt

Paste the Business Brief first (`../01-foundation/business-brief.md`), then
this, then attach the photos.

> I'm attaching photos from a job we finished this week. Here's the context:
>
> - Job type: [interior repaint / exterior repaint / cabinets / repair]
> - Location: [neighbourhood, not street address]
> - What made this one notable: [the actual thing — a difficult substrate, a
>   colour decision, a repair nobody expected, a tight schedule]
> - Products used: [brand and line]
>
> Write me five social captions from this job:
>
> 1. **The craft note.** One specific technical thing we did and why it matters
>    in five years. Assume the reader doesn't know the terminology; explain it
>    without condescending.
> 2. **The before/after.** State what the surface was doing when we arrived and
>    what it took to fix it. No adjectives about how amazing the result is —
>    let the photo do that.
> 3. **The homeowner question.** A question we actually get asked, answered
>    honestly, using this job as the example.
> 4. **The mistake to avoid.** Something homeowners or other contractors get
>    wrong on this kind of work, stated without naming or sneering at anyone.
> 5. **The short one.** Under 30 words. Just what it is, where, and what product.
>
> Constraints: no exclamation points, no superlatives, never call our own work
> premium or luxury, no "transformation Tuesday" or similar. Write the way a
> tradesman with fifteen years in explains something to a neighbour in the
> driveway. Vary the openings — do not start more than one with the same word.

---

## Where each one goes

| Caption | Use |
|---|---|
| Craft note | Google Post + Instagram |
| Before/after | Instagram + Facebook |
| Homeowner question | Add to the GBP Q&A section, and Facebook |
| Mistake to avoid | Instagram + LinkedIn if you're doing designer outreach |
| Short one | Instagram Story, or a second Google Post mid-week |

One post a day is not the target. Three a week, consistently, beats seven a week
for a month and then nothing.

## Hashtag bank

Rotate — using the identical block every time gets a post's reach suppressed.
Pick roughly 8–12 per post, weighted toward the local and specific ones.

a million posts and no local relevance. They bring bots, not neighbours.

## What to photograph, so there's something to post

Give this list to the crew. It's four photos a job, not a photo shoot.

1. **Before**, wide, from the doorway or the corner of the yard.
2. **Prep in progress** — protection down, or scraped and primed surfaces. This
   is the one that separates you from every competitor's feed, and almost nobody
   takes it.
3. **A detail** — a cut line, a caulked joint, a cabinet edge.
4. **After**, from the exact same position as the before shot.

Shoot in daylight. Turn on the room lights for interiors. Don't use flash.
Hold the phone level with the wall, not tilted.

## Franchise note

A new operator inherits this prompt and the photo list unchanged. What they
replace is the local hashtag block and the neighbourhood names, both of which
come from `service_area` in `config/business-profile.yml`.
