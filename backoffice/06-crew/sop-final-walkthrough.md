# SOP — Final Walkthrough & Closeout

One page. This is the step that decides whether you get a review, a referral, and
paid without friction.

> **The standard:** we find the punch list, not the homeowner. If they point at
> something we should have caught, we have already lost the review even if we fix
> it in thirty seconds.

---

## Checklist

**1. Crew does its own walkthrough first, in raking light, before the homeowner
is invited.** Flashlight held flat against the wall. *Done correctly:* every
defect on the list is one we found ourselves.

**2. Fix the punch list before the homeowner walk, not during it.** *Done
correctly:* the homeowner's walk is a confirmation, not an inspection.

**3. Restore the space.** Furniture back where it was, plates and screws back on,
hardware rehung, floors cleaned, drop cloths and debris gone. *Done correctly:*
the room looks like it did, only painted.

**4. Walk the homeowner room by room, in daylight where possible.** Ask directly:
"Is there anything here you want me to look at?" *Done correctly:* you invited
the criticism rather than waiting for it.

**5. Write down anything they raise, in front of them**, and say when it will be
handled. *Done correctly:* nothing is carried in memory.

**6. Hand over the paint schedule** — brand, product line, colour name, code, and
sheen for every surface, plus where any leftover material is stored. *Done
correctly:* they can touch up in two years without calling to ask.

**7. Explain the cure window.** What they can and cannot do for the next few
days — washing, hanging, furniture against walls, tape on trim.

**8. State the warranty in plain words**, matching what the proposal said. Do not
improvise coverage on the driveway.

**9. Take the after photos** — same angles as the before shots. Ask permission
before using any of them publicly. *Done correctly:* you have a matched pair for
the case study and the Google post.

**10. Present the final invoice and ask for payment**, then tell them the review
text is coming this evening. *Done correctly:* the review request that arrives
later is expected, not a surprise.

---

## Common mistakes

Walking the homeowner before the crew's own walk. Fixing punch items while the
homeowner watches, which reads as sloppiness even when the fix is perfect.
Leaving the paint schedule "for later" and never sending it. Inventing a warranty
term that's more generous than the proposal. Photographing from different angles
than the before shots, which makes the pair useless. Leaving without asking for
payment, then chasing it for three weeks.

# PROCEDIMIENTO — Recorrido Final y Cierre

> **BORRADOR — requiere revisión de un hablante nativo antes de usarse.**

**El estándar:** los defectos los encontramos nosotros, no el cliente. Si el
cliente señala algo que debimos ver, ya perdimos la reseña aunque lo arreglemos
en treinta segundos.

**1. El equipo hace su propio recorrido primero**, con luz rasante y linterna
pegada a la pared, antes de invitar al cliente.

**2. Corrija la lista de pendientes antes del recorrido con el cliente**, no
durante.

**3. Restaure el espacio:** muebles en su lugar, placas y tornillos puestos,
herrajes colgados, pisos limpios, lonas y basura fuera.

**4. Recorra con el cliente habitación por habitación**, con luz de día si es
posible. Pregunte directamente: "¿Hay algo aquí que quiere que revise?"

**5. Anote frente al cliente todo lo que mencione** y diga cuándo se resolverá.

**6. Entregue la ficha de pintura:** marca, línea de producto, nombre y código de
color, y acabado de cada superficie, más dónde quedó el material sobrante.

**7. Explique el tiempo de curado:** qué se puede y no se puede hacer los
próximos días.

**8. Explique la garantía en palabras sencillas**, igual a lo que dice la
propuesta. No improvise coberturas.

**9. Tome las fotos finales** desde los mismos ángulos que las iniciales. Pida
permiso antes de usarlas públicamente.

**10. Presente la factura final y pida el pago**, y avise que esta tarde llegará
un mensaje pidiendo una reseña.

**Errores comunes:** recorrer con el cliente antes del recorrido interno;
arreglar pendientes frente al cliente; no enviar la ficha de pintura; prometer
una garantía distinta a la propuesta; fotos desde ángulos distintos; irse sin
pedir el pago.
