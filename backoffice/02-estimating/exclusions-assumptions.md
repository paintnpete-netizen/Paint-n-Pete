# Exclusions & Assumptions

Attach to every residential proposal as section 5. Write once, reuse forever.

> **Have this reviewed once by an attorney or your insurance agent before it
> becomes standard.** It is written as plain-English clarity, not as legal
> protection, and it has not been reviewed by anyone qualified to say whether it
> protects you under Florida law. That review is a one-time cost against every
> dispute it prevents.

Most lost margin is not a pricing error. It is the gap between what the client
assumed was included and what the proposal actually said.

---

## The block

**A few things worth putting in writing**

So there are no surprises in either direction, here is what this price covers and
where we would need to talk before going further.

**What we may find once we start.** Wood rot and damaged substrate often only
become visible after washing and scraping. The same is true of failed previous
coatings and hidden water damage. We do not carry those repairs in this price
because we cannot see them yet. If we find them, we will stop, show you, and
give you the cost and schedule impact in writing before we continue.

**Homes built before 1978.** These require lead-safe work practices, which
affect containment, cleanup, and schedule. If your home falls in this range, we
will confirm the approach before starting.

**Not included unless listed in the scope above.** Wallpaper removal, popcorn or
textured ceiling repair, and moving furniture beyond light items. Each is real
work and we are glad to quote it — it just needs to be priced rather than
assumed.

**Materials you supply.** We are happy to apply paint you have purchased, but we
cannot warrant the finish of a product we did not specify. Our warranty covers
our workmanship and the coatings we select.

**Approvals.** If you are in an HOA or condo association, color and schedule
approval is yours to obtain. We will supply whatever documentation they need.

**Access.** We need working power and water on site, and access to the areas in
the scope on the days we have scheduled them.

**Weather.** Exterior work depends on temperature, humidity, and rain. We will
not spray or topcoat in conditions that compromise the finish. This can move a
schedule, and we will keep you posted when it does.

**Cure time.** Paint is dry to the touch long before it has cured. We will tell
you when a room can be used and when it can take furniture against a wall or a
cabinet door closed against a frame. Those are different dates, and rushing the
second one is the most common way a good finish gets damaged.

Roughly 340 words. Keep it under 350 — past that people stop reading, which
defeats the purpose.

The tone target is a homeowner reading this as helpful clarity rather than a
contractor covering himself. If any line starts sounding like a contract,
rewrite it in the words you would use standing in their kitchen.
