# Objection Responses

Written to be said out loud in a driveway, not read off a screen.

**Read these aloud before you use them.** If a line doesn't sound like something
you would actually say, rewrite it. A script you can't deliver naturally is
worse than no script — people hear the seam.

Every one of them anchors to the same three things: prep quality, product
selection, and what the finish looks like in five years. Confident, never
defensive, never desperate.

---

## "We got a quote for $3,000 less."

> That's probably a real quote — the difference is what's inside it. Ask them how
> many coats, what primer, and how many hours they've allowed for prep. If
> they're matching what I've written down, take it, genuinely. Most of the time
> you'll find the gap is prep hours. That's the part that decides whether you're
> doing this again in four years.

Never disparage the other contractor. Handing them a way to compare properly is
more persuasive than any defence of your own number, and if the other quote
really is equivalent, you want them to take it.

---

## "We need to think about it."

> Of course — it's real money and you should. Is there something specific you're
> weighing? Sometimes it's the price, sometimes it's the timing, sometimes it's a
> line item that didn't quite make sense. Any of those I can help with. And if
> you just need a week, take the week.

The question is the whole response. "We need to think about it" is almost always
standing in for something narrower, and you can't address what you can't hear.

---

## "Can you do it for [lower number]?"

> I can't get there and still do it the way I've described it. What I can do is
> take something out — if we drop [SCOPE ITEM], the number moves. But I'd rather
> give you less work at the right standard than all of it at a lower one. Which
> would you rather do?

Scope comes out or the price stays. Never the same work for less money — that
tells them the original number was padded, and they'll be right to wonder.

---

## "We're going to wait until after the holidays."

> That's fine — January's actually good for interior work, humidity's down and
> cure times behave. Only thing worth knowing is that my January tends to fill up
> in November. Want me to pencil you in? Nothing binding, it just holds the spot.

Agree with the delay, then give a real reason the timing is fine, then make it
easy to hold the slot. No pressure, because there's genuinely nothing to pressure
about.

---

## "My brother-in-law says he can do it."

> He might do a fine job. If he's done cabinets before and he owns a sprayer,
> honestly, let him. Where it usually goes sideways is prep and the things that
> only show in raking light. If he takes it on and you're not happy with where it
> lands, call me — no hard feelings either way.

Do not compete with family. You will lose, and you'll look small doing it. Being
gracious here is what gets the call in six months, which is a real and frequent
outcome.
