# The Three-Touch Follow-Up

Quotes go quiet because nobody remembered to check, not because the price was
wrong. Three touches, then stop.

| Timing | Purpose | Tone |
|---|---|---|
| Day 3 | Confirm it arrived, offer to walk any line item | Helpful, no pressure |
| Day 8 | Add value — product, prep, or a similar project | Expert, not salesman |
| Day 21 | Clean close — hold the slot or release it | Direct, unbothered |

**Two standing rules: never offer a discount, and never apologise for the
price.** A discount at day 8 tells them the first number was invented, and it
teaches every future client to wait you out.

---

## Day 3

**Subject:** Your proposal

> [NAME] — just confirming the proposal reached you on [DATE]. They occasionally
> land in spam.
>
> No rush on a decision. If any line item is unclear — why a surface needs the
> prep it does, or why I specified one product over another — I'm happy to walk
> you through it on the phone. That usually takes ten minutes, and it's the
> reason the proposal is itemized in the first place.
>
> Noah

---

## Day 8

**Subject:** One thing about your [PROJECT]

> [NAME] — something I didn't spell out in the proposal.
>
> [SPECIFIC OBSERVATION. Example: On a house your age, with the west-side
> exposure yours has, the failure point is almost always the caulk at the trim
> joints rather than the field of the siding. That's why the prep line looks
> heavy next to the paint line — it's where the five-year result actually gets
> decided.]
>
> Worth knowing whoever you end up hiring. Happy to talk it through if it's
> useful.
>
> Noah

The observation has to be real and specific to their house. A generic version of
this email is worse than not sending one, because it proves you weren't paying
attention. "Worth knowing whoever you end up hiring" is the line that makes it
land — it's advice, not a pitch, and it's true.

---

## Day 21

**Subject:** Holding your slot?

> [NAME] — I'm scheduling into [MONTH] now and I've been holding a slot with
> your project on it.
>
> If you'd still like to go ahead, say so and I'll firm up dates. If you've gone
> another direction or shelved it for now, that's completely fine — just let me
> know and I'll release it to someone else.
>
> Either answer works. I'd rather know than guess.
>
> Noah

"Either answer works" is what makes people actually reply. It removes the
awkwardness of saying no, and a clean no is worth far more than another month of
silence — it tells you your real close rate.

---

## Recording it

Enter the proposal send date in the leads tracker the moment it goes out. That
date drives all three touches, and workflow 2 in `../07-automation/` fires the
reminders off it.

**Log the outcome either way — won or lost.** That record is the only way your
close rate becomes visible, and close rate is what tells you whether a quiet
month is a lead problem or a pricing problem. They have completely different
fixes.
